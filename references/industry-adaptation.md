# Industry Adaptation Matrix

Keep research, evidence, approval, and measurement controls constant. Configure taxonomy, page modules, conversions, proof, and compliance for the business.

## Industry profile

Capture: business/revenue model; conversions; customer types and buying roles; product/service taxonomy and attributes; sales cycle; geography/language; inventory/availability/locations; expertise/proof/credentials; regulated claims; margins/strategic offers/seasonality; CMS/templates/structured-data constraints.

## Page module selection

### Ecommerce and DTC

Use home, collections/categories, products, use-case/audience pages, comparisons, buying guides, editorial, brand/story, policies, and support. Map variants, availability, merchandising, filters, reviews, and Product/Offer data.

### SaaS and software

Use home, product/features, solutions, industries, use cases, integrations, alternatives/comparisons, pricing, security, docs, templates/tools, case studies, glossary, and resources. Map trial/demo/signup conversions and avoid unsupported comparison claims.

### B2B, manufacturing, and distribution

Use capabilities, product families, applications, industries, specifications, certifications, documentation, case studies, distributors/locations, RFQ/contact, and resources. Preserve part-number/spec discovery and real regional availability.

### Local and professional services

Use service, location, practitioner/team, case/review, pricing or consultation, FAQ, and resources. Create service × location pages only for real service areas with distinct useful local evidence. Avoid doorway grids.

### Education and training

Use programs/courses, subjects, audience, outcomes, curriculum, faculty, admissions/enrollment, schedules, accreditation, student proof, and guides. Keep dates, claims, prices, and eligibility current.

### Healthcare, finance, legal, and YMYL

Require authors/reviewers where appropriate, credentials, review dates, primary evidence, careful claims, jurisdiction, disclosures, and qualified-human review. Do not generate diagnoses, guarantees, or unsupported outcomes. SEO opportunity never overrides safety or regulation.

### Publisher, media, and knowledge brands

Use topic hubs, articles, evergreen explainers, news, authors, newsletters, tools/data, and editorial/corrections policy. Control archives, tags, pagination, syndicated content, and freshness. Do not mass-create thin tag pages.

### Marketplaces and directories

Use category, subcategory, provider/listing, location, comparison, editorial, and trust/safety modules. Index programmatic combinations only with sufficient inventory, uniqueness, demand, and crawl controls.

## Reusable decision tests

Before approving a page type, ask:

1. Is there distinct user intent?
2. Can the brand provide unique inventory, expertise, proof, data, or utility?
3. Is it materially different from existing owners?
4. Can it remain accurate and maintained?
5. Does it have an internal-link role and conversion path?
6. Would it be useful without search traffic?

If several answers are no, strengthen an existing page instead.

## Schema selection

Use only schema matching visible content and actual entities. Candidates include Organization/LocalBusiness, WebSite, WebPage, BreadcrumbList, Product/Offer, Service, Article/BlogPosting, Course, Event, Person, SoftwareApplication, and FAQPage where eligible. Validate current requirements; never promise rich results.
